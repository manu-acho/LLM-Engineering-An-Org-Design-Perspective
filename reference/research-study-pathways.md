# Organisation research: foundation and advanced study

[Research reading guide](literature-guide.md) · [Project bibliography](bibliography.md) · [Meet Harbour](../HARBOUR.md)

## Start from a business problem

Harbour's delivery-discrepancy service connects customer service, warehouse investigation, transport evidence and finance remedies. An AI-generated answer can affect all four. The nine research chapters explain how to investigate those effects. The following routes use the same material at different depths; no prior organisation theory is assumed for the foundation route.

A **theory** is an organised explanation that directs attention to particular relationships. A **concept** names something worth distinguishing, such as a dependency or decision right. An **empirical study** investigates observations. A **conceptual paper** develops an argument without necessarily testing it through new observations. A **proposition** states a relationship to be examined; it is not a finding simply because it sounds plausible.

These distinctions matter when reading AI claims. A proposed governance framework can sharpen a design without proving it effective. A measured productivity change can be informative while remaining specific to a population, task and period.

## Foundation route

Read each chapter's opening case, definitions, worked example and exercise before following the additional publications. Return to unfamiliar technical terms through the [glossary](glossary.md). The engineering modules and paired notebooks provide a parallel way to inspect selected mechanisms; research proposals are not automatically implemented software features.

| Sequence | Reading | Learning outcome | Small piece of evidence to prepare |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Design](research/01-organisation-design.md) and [coordination](research/02-coordination.md) | Explain how changing one task affects other work | A map of activities, dependencies and receiving needs |
| 2 | [Decisions](research/03-decisions.md) and [governance](research/08-governance-and-accountability.md) | Separate evidence, recommendation, authority and answerability | A decision-rights table with exception and correction routes |
| 3 | [Implementation](research/04-implementation.md) and [routines](research/05-routines.md) | Distinguish an intended arrangement from repeated practice | Two rival explanations for a pilot result |
| 4 | [Technology in practice](research/06-technology-in-practice.md) and [infrastructure](research/07-infrastructure-and-standards.md) | Explain why a capability depends on actors and shared foundations | A field-definition example and maintenance responsibility |
| 5 | [Process architecture](research/09-process-architecture.md) | Connect a technical run to a business outcome | Separate start and end conditions for each |

**Cumulative assignment.** Prepare a short design brief for Harbour's evidence assistant. Define the customer problem, affected work, evidence requirements, decision rights, maintenance responsibilities and intended outcome. Include an ambiguous delivery case that cannot safely proceed. Explain what the service should do next and who takes responsibility.

A satisfactory brief explains the concepts in ordinary language, applies at least three distinct research perspectives and separates a design proposal from evidence of benefit. It should not treat the same approval button as proof of coordination, control and accountability. Each claim needs its own reasoning.

## Advanced route

The advanced route builds on that brief by asking how its claims could be challenged. Start with a chapter's cited publications, check the access notes and read the appropriate original text before developing a detailed interpretation. An abstract or bibliographic record is insufficient for page-specific claims. The bibliography identifies the version consulted where a manuscript was used.

| Inquiry | Task | What makes the argument stronger |
|---|---|---|
| Compare explanations | Analyse one handoff through coordination and routines theory | State what each explains and what it leaves unresolved |
| Examine authority | Compare formal rights with observed influence over evidence and options | Consider expertise, time pressure and policy as rival explanations |
| Study infrastructure | Follow a category or unit change across teams and versions | Trace maintenance and local repair, not only the interface specification |
| Evaluate a result | Compare the AI-at-work study with Harbour's proposed pilot | Identify differences in population, task, outcome and operating conditions |
| Design research | Propose a bounded comparative or longitudinal study | Specify cases, data sources, missing evidence and a result that could contradict the proposition |

**Advanced assignment.** Develop a research proposal from the design brief. State one question, compare two theoretical interpretations, formulate a contestable proposition and explain a feasible method. Define the unit of analysis, meaning the entity being studied: a decision, handoff, case, team or service. Explain why that choice fits the question.

Include a method for checking the gap between prescribed, configured and performed work. For example, compare a procedure, selected configuration, event records and observations of disputed cases. Explain the limits of each source. A log can show an event under its recording rules; it cannot establish the complete practice or the legitimacy of a decision.

The proposal should address access, consent, confidentiality and the consequences of studying workers where relevant. These are concrete research-design considerations, not a claim that a fictional classroom exercise requires a formal field study.

## Read a study before borrowing its conclusion

For each publication, record its question, theoretical argument, method, setting, principal result and limitations. Distinguish a result reported by the authors from an interpretation developed for Harbour. Ask what evidence would be needed to transfer the claim to another setting.

For a quantitative result, identify the measured output, denominator, comparison and uncertainty. For a qualitative study, examine case selection, data sources, interpretation and rival accounts. For a review, examine how the literature was assembled and what the synthesis can support. For a conceptual framework, examine its assumptions and the relationships it proposes.

The objective is to make a defensible claim at an appropriate scope. More citations do not compensate for an undefined outcome or a missing link between evidence and conclusion. The [bibliography](bibliography.md) is a starting collection for that work, with access boundaries kept visible.
