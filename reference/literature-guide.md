# Organisation design research: a reading guide

[Course index](../README.md) · [Start here](../START-HERE.md)

## Start with a problem in the work

An AI assistant can prepare a better answer while leaving the business problem unresolved. Someone still supplies evidence, interprets a recommendation, accepts a handoff and deals with exceptions. Literature from organisation design research helps explain those connections.

The six chapters below teach that literature in the context of AI integration. They can be read here, without a separate course or a visit to a research repository. Each develops a practical problem, explains concepts before using them, connects the arguments of different authors, and returns to a worked business case.

Harbour, the packaging distributor used throughout, is fictional. Its cases illustrate reasoning. They do not report observed deployments or measured benefits.

## How the argument develops

Organisation design first identifies the division of work and the need to reconnect it. Coordination theory then examines the particular dependencies and the means of managing them. Decision theory follows the premises, alternatives and authority through which action becomes possible.

Implementation research asks how a generic capability meets local requirements. Routines research examines the difference between a represented process and recurrent work. Technology and materiality research explains why a feature's consequences depend on actors, goals and processes of use.

The perspectives overlap without being interchangeable. An approval can be a coordination gate, an allocated decision opportunity and an artefact in recurrent work. Each interpretation answers a different question. None, alone, proves meaningful review or a better business outcome.

## Concepts, examples and citations

The chapters explain the organisational concepts needed to examine AI-supported work. Their cases and exercises supply the context for applying those concepts; no separate research document is required to follow the argument.

Author-date citations link to reference entries within each chapter. The entries identify the scholarly publications and provide publication links where available. The explanations, worked examples and comparisons are included in the chapters themselves.

The literature includes conceptual arguments, simulation models and empirical studies of particular organisations. Those forms of evidence answer different questions. An enterprise-system or healthcare study can help frame an AI investigation without establishing that an AI service will produce the same outcome. The chapters keep that distinction beside the relevant claim. Harbour and the other teaching examples are fictional.

## Match the evidence to the claim

The following path is a teaching synthesis across the reviews, not a single model attributable to one author.

| Evidence | A defensible claim | A claim it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Prompt, schema or workflow diagram | A represented arrangement or instruction | What was deployed or followed |
| Inspected settings and integrations | A selected technical arrangement | Recurrent use or meaningful review |
| Operating roles, procedures and deployment records | An arrangement was put into operation | That participants can use it effectively |
| Situated accounts and observation | What particular actors understand or perceive | A universal shared interpretation |
| Repeated observations and traces | Particular patterns of work, with stated coverage limits | The complete practice from logs alone |
| Comparative or longitudinal outcome evidence | Defined consequences for a population and period | An automatic effect of the technology in other settings |

A technical test can be valuable within its scope. A version check can demonstrate that an old proposal is blocked under tested conditions. Evidence of organisational benefit requires a further account of how the arrangement enters work, what changes and how those changes are assessed.
