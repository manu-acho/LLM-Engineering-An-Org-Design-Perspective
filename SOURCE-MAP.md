## Engineering influences

Ed Donner's three courses provide important influences on the choice of engineering topics and the progression from models to agents and operational services:

- [LLM Engineering](https://github.com/ed-donner/llm_engineering): generation, retrieval, evaluation, data and adaptation.
- [Agents](https://github.com/ed-donner/agents): loops, orchestration, frameworks, state and capability interfaces.
- [AI FDE: The Forward Deployed Engineer & Agentic AI Architect](https://github.com/ed-donner/undercover): business requirements, harnesses, evaluation, durable work, security, enterprise context, deployment and controlled improvement.

These courses are optional further learning. The standalone pilot does not require their videos, code, datasets or accounts. Credit for the original courses and repository contributions belongs to Ed Donner and their credited contributors. This edition does not imply affiliation or endorsement.

## Original examples

The pilot's word-count model, evidence preparation, request construction, checks and fictional quantities are independently written teaching examples. They are not copied implementations of UnderCover or the other course projects. The examples state exactly what runs and what each result can establish.

The third course's implementation briefs informed the planned operational questions. A brief is a statement of intended behaviour, not verification that a deployed system fulfils it. No UnderCover deployment, payment operation, model call or improvement round was executed for this pilot.

## Scholarly foundation

Six [research chapters](reference/literature-guide.md) explain the organisation design concepts within the course and cite the scholarly publications. Their cases are fictional. Concepts from enterprise-system, healthcare and other studies are applied with their original setting boundaries intact.

## Publication status

This edition currently publishes one standalone pilot module and the six research chapters. The [curriculum](CURRICULUM.md) marks later modules as planned. A working static reader does not mean that those future teaching units or cloud implementations are complete.
