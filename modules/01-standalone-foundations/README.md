# Module 1: Evidence, language models and work

[Course home](../../README.md) · [Setup](../../START-HERE.md) · [Lab guide](../../LAB-GUIDE.md)

A customer reports ten boxes against an order for twelve. The module follows that discrepancy through a small program, a learned language model and a proposed investigation service. Each example supplies its inputs and explains what its result can establish.

## The complete route

1. [A model, an application and a business problem](01-model-and-application.md)
2. [Reading and running a small Python program](02-python-first-steps.md)
3. [Train a tiny language model](03-learning-and-generation.md)
4. [Prepare evidence and construct a request](04-evidence-pipeline.md)
5. [From the tiny model to a neural LLM](05-neural-models.md)
6. [Design the work around the contribution](06-business-design.md)
7. [Exercises, failure cases and worked answers](07-assessment.md)

The business route follows Lessons 1, 3, 5, 6 and 7. The implementation route follows all seven. No previous programming knowledge or other course is required. The research chapters explain relevant concepts in greater depth, but the main argument and exercises are contained here.

## What completion means

The module should leave a learner able to distinguish model from application, calculate a small conditional distribution, follow a function through its inputs and outputs, preserve evidence status, explain the difference between count-based and neural models, and propose a bounded service design with a meaningful comparison.

Seven notebooks follow the lessons in order. The [study workspace guide](../../STUDY-WORKSPACE.md) explains how to open them and select the prepared environment. No API keys are needed.

Two downloadable files contain the complete local lab and its checks. They need Python 3.10 or later and no extra packages, model downloads, accounts or network calls. Later modules introduce external integrations separately.
